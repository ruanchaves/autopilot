---
name: recruiter-outreach
description: A standalone LinkedIn recruiter pipeline, separate from job applications. Finds recruiters who place the user's target role from their region (config.yaml), nearshore or worldwide, through people search, feed posts, VC talent networks and colleagues of proven leads, and ranks each 0-100 on five questions - hiring in the user's region or worldwide, active on LinkedIn, posting the target role, posting the target pay, and recruiting for clients that pay it (funded product companies and boutique search, not staff augmentation, US-IT vendors or task platforms). Companies whose highest pay ever quoted to the user is under the floor are held back; linkedin-inbox records the quotes. A company lane works alongside: funded companies hiring the target role - from job-source-sweep's roles and from a registry built out of funding news, VC portfolios and accelerator batches, whose ATS boards are read for remote roles open to the user's region - are reached through their in-house recruiter and hiring manager, scored on whether that person owns the role, with a share of the daily budget reserved and both lanes compared on recruiters quoting the target pay. Each run spends the day's budget - 20 new connection requests with a custom note, then InMails while Premium credits last, plus a plain message to every 1st-degree lead, which never counts toward the 20 - and keeps prospecting while budget remains. Messages are short, written for that person from their posts and tone, never state pay, and close with an open door (open to new roles, or the CV) rather than a call request. Every lead remembers the search that found it, so the skill learns which searches work. Nobody is contacted twice; current employers never. Results show on the role explorer's Outreach view. First use runs an interactive setup, rerun on "set up" or "reconfigure". Use when the user wants to find recruiters or hiring managers, build or refresh the recruiter database, rank leads, send today's connection requests or InMails, or review outreach. It does not apply to jobs (job-autopilot) and does not answer replies (linkedin-inbox).

# Recruiter outreach

A pipeline of its own: discover recruiters, score them, contact the best ones within the daily limits. Nothing here is tied to an application. The user authorised sending connection requests and InMails when they set this up; discovery never sends anything.

**Paths**
- **Skill:** `<repo>/recruiter-outreach` (`<rc>`), where `<repo>` is the autopilot repo (here `D:\dev\autopilot`)
- **Script:** `<rc>/scripts/recruiters.py`. It reuses job-source-sweep's helpers, so it needs the sibling `job-source-sweep` folder.
- **Data:** `<home>/recruiters/`, where `<home>` is `home` in `config.yaml`. The company lane also reads, never writes, job-source-sweep's `<home>/runs/` and `classifications.json` and job-autopilot's `<home>/autopilot/scores.json` and `ledger.json`; its own funded-company registry is `<home>/recruiters/funded_companies.json`.
- **Candidate snapshot:** `candidate` in `config.yaml` (pitch, stories, location), plus `<home>/fit-rubric.md` and `resume.base` for the facts.

**Running the script.** With the runner from `config.yaml`: for `python: wsl`, `wsl.exe -e python3 /mnt/<repo>/recruiter-outreach/scripts/recruiters.py <cmd>` (from Git Bash, first `export MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1`). `--home` defaults to `home` in `config.yaml`. Below, `recruiters <cmd>` is short for that.

**Settings** come from `<repo>/config.yaml`. The sections this skill reads:

| Section / key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `role.title` | The role the lead's posted jobs are matched against. |
| `pay.target_usd_month` | The pay the lead's posted jobs are matched against, and the bar for a `quoted_target` outcome. |
| `pay.skip_below_usd_month` | The pay floor. A company whose highest quote in `pay_ceilings.json` is under it is held just below `min_score`. |
| `candidate` | Country, region and aliases (what "hiring in the candidate's region" means), pitch, location pitch, stories, stack, employment types, native language. Handed to both subagents as `candidate`. |
| `banned_companies` | The user's current employers and clients. Their recruiters are never contacted. |
| `recruiter_outreach.connect_per_day` | New connection requests per UTC day. The user's hard limit, and the day's target: the run isn't finished until they are all used. It limits connection requests only: messages to people who are already 1st-degree connections never count toward it. |
| `recruiter_outreach.inmail_per_day` | A second guard on InMails; the real bound is the credits LinkedIn shows. The run isn't finished until the credits (or this cap) are used. |
| `recruiter_outreach.message_per_day` | Plain messages to leads who are already 1st-degree connections. Empty or `null` (the default) means no daily cap; `0` means none at all. A separate channel: it is not the 20-a-day connection budget and never spends from it. |
| `recruiter_outreach.min_score` | Leads below this are not contacted. |
| `recruiter_outreach.max_per_company` | How many people may ever be contacted at one company or agency (default 3). `<home>/recruiters/companies.json` remembers every company reached so far, across runs and including the old autopilot contacts; `plan` holds back further leads there and `check` refuses them. |
| `recruiter_outreach.stale_days` | A lead's signals are probed again after this long. |
| `recruiter_outreach.people_queries`, `post_queries` | The starting LinkedIn search phrasings, written for the candidate's role and region. Each is rerun at most once a week. They are a seed, not a ceiling: when they are spent, the skill invents more (step 1). |
| `recruiter_outreach.required_terms` | Words every discovery pass must search with, such as `nearshore`. Each discovery subagent runs at least one people and one post phrasing containing each term, and `ingest` reports `required_terms_missing` when one didn't. |
| `recruiter_outreach.company_lane_share` | Share of the day's connection requests + InMails reserved for the company lane (default 0.25; 0 turns the lane off). Slots the lane can't fill go to recruiters and vice versa, so the split never leaves budget unspent. |
| `recruiter_outreach.company_lane_batches`, `company_lane_per_round`, `company_lane_cooldown_days`, `company_lane_title_terms` | How many sweep batches the targets come from (14), how many companies one discovery round works (8), how long before a searched company may be searched again (30 days), and which posting-title words count as the target role (default: the words of `role.title`). |
| `recruiter_outreach.company_lane_min_score` | The contact line for lane leads, on their own rubric (default 55). |
| `recruiter_outreach.company_lane_research_days`, `company_lane_research_per_round`, `company_lane_funding_days`, `company_lane_min_round_usd`, `company_lane_board_refresh_hours` | The funded-company registry: funding research is due every 7 days (or sooner when the lane runs short of targets), checks 30 companies a pass, counts a round from the last 180 days as recent, skips announced rounds under $3M, and re-reads each company's ATS board after 20 hours. |
| `recruiter_outreach.cta_styles` | How first messages close (default `open`, `cv`; see `references/message.md`). `plan` gives each lead one style, spread evenly, and `recruiters queries` compares their acceptance and reply rates. None of them asks for a call. |

## The run, in one sentence

Repeat **discover → score → contact** until `recruiters status` says `done: true` (today's connection requests are all used, the InMail credits or cap are used, and every contactable 1st-degree lead has had its plain message), or LinkedIn blocks, or the user says stop. An empty pile is never a reason to stop: it means discover more. `status` reports `needed`, the number of leads today's remaining connection requests and InMails can still take; `contactable_now`, the number of uncontacted leads at or above `min_score` at companies still under `max_per_company` that need one of those (2nd and 3rd degree); and `first_degree_pending`, contactable leads who are already 1st-degree connections. Whenever `contactable_now < needed`, discover before contacting.

**Two budgets, never mixed.** The 20 a day (`connect_per_day`) is a limit on *new connection requests*, nothing else. A lead who is already a 1st-degree connection gets a plain message, which spends no connection request and does not count toward the 20; those messages have no daily cap unless `message_per_day` sets one. So a run with all 20 requests used still messages every 1st-degree lead in the pile, and a 1st-degree lead never takes a slot a connection request could have used.

Run `jobsweep config` (the sweep's script) and read `setup_done`. Run this section when `recruiter-outreach` is not in it, or when the user asks to "set up" or "reconfigure" recruiter outreach. Otherwise skip to step 1.

2. Say in one line that this skill sends LinkedIn connection requests and InMails on the user's behalf within caps they set now, that discovery alone sends nothing, and that `/recruiter-outreach reconfigure` or editing `config.yaml` changes it later. Then call **AskUserQuestion**, up to 4 questions per call. Skip any question the request already answers; when rerunning, put the current value first marked "(current)".

**Call 2**
5. *Strictness*: "Contact leads scoring at least…?" Options: 55 of 100, meaning region and role plus a client type or pay that fits (Recommended); 70, meaning proven target-pay clients only; 40, meaning anyone plausible. → `recruiter_outreach.min_score`
6. *Languages*: no question. Messages are always written in English; the only exception is an existing conversation thread held in another language, which continues in that language (`references/message.md`, **Language**).

3. **Write the search phrasings.** From `role.title`, `candidate.country`, `region`, `region_aliases` and `native_language`, write eight `people_queries` (recruiter nouns × role synonyms × region terms; at least three aimed at the clients that pay the target, such as "talent partner AI startups <region>", "founding engineer recruiter", "talent partner venture capital portfolio <role>"; at most one in the native language; at least one with each `required_terms` word, such as "nearshore" for a LATAM candidate) and six `post_queries` (the words a recruiter would use in the hiring post, half of them with an annual USD band at or above the target: "hiring founding <role> <region>", "<12×target/1000>k-<18×target/1000>k remote <role> <region>"). Show them and ask one question: "Use these search phrasings?" Options: Yes (Recommended); "let me edit them" (Other). → `recruiter_outreach.people_queries`, `post_queries`. They are a seed: the skill invents more when they are spent.
4. Seed the contacts memory so nobody is written to twice: if `<home>/autopilot/contacts.json` exists, run `recruiters import-contacts --file <home>/autopilot/contacts.json`. Also offer to import the user's LinkedIn connections export (CSV) the same way if they have one.
5. Edit `config.yaml` key by key with the Edit tool, keeping its comments, and add `recruiter-outreach` to `setup_done`. Show the saved settings as a short list (`stale_days` 60 and `gap_seconds` keep their defaults). Then continue into the run if the invocation asked for one; otherwise offer to start. When every cap is 0, a run is one discovery round plus the report.

## 1. Discover

Run this when the user asks to find or refresh recruiters, and in every outreach round where `contactable_now < needed`. The pile loses about half of its raw leads to the score cut and to leads already in conversation, so collect about twice `needed` per round; `discover-plan` sets `max_leads` to that.

1. Run `recruiters refresh-boards` (it reads the ATS boards of the funded-company registry and skips boards read in the last `company_lane_board_refresh_hours`), then `recruiters discover-plan`. It lists the configured people and post queries not run in the last week, `exhausted` (true when none are left), `queries_tried` (every phrasing already run, so you don't repeat one), `deeper` (phrasings run this week whose result pages 3 and beyond are unread), `snowball_companies` (only firms proven to place at the target pay: agencies in `pay_ceilings.json` at or above it, then employers of leads who quoted or posted it), `vc_funds` (the VC and accelerator portfolios job-source-sweep reads; their talent partners recruit for funded companies), `known_low_ceiling` (companies whose highest quote is under the floor; skip their recruiters), `company_lane` (the lane's reserved slots, its contactable leads and `short_by`) and `company_targets` (this round's companies for the lane, only when it is short), `funding_research` (`due` when the registry wasn't researched in `company_lane_research_days` or the lane is short of targets), `seeds` (role, region, recruiter, client, pay and hashtag terms taken from config.yaml, plus the `language_rule`), `required_terms` (each with how often it was run this week), `known_profiles`, `reprobe`, `needed`, `max_leads`, and the `result` path.
2. **Build the query list for this round.** Take the configured queries first. When they don't cover `max_leads` (and always when `exhausted` is true), **invent new phrasings yourself** and add them. Running out of configured phrasings is never a reason to stop prospecting. Ways to make new ones, all of which the discovery subagent knows how to run:
   - **New people and post phrasings**, at least six per round, that aren't in `queries_tried`. Vary every axis of `seeds`: role synonyms (for an AI engineer: ML engineer, LLM engineer, GenAI, applied AI, NLP engineer, machine learning, agents, RAG), region terms (country, region, each alias, "nearshore", "anywhere", "worldwide", "remote-first"), recruiter nouns (recruiter, talent partner, sourcer, headhunter, executive search, "founding engineer recruiter", "we're hiring", "hiring for", "open role"), client terms (YC, Series A, seed, VC-backed, founding, AI-native, applied research), and pay terms written as the recruiters who pay the target write them: annual USD bands ("$120k", "130k-180k", "$150k") and "equity", not "contractor", "B2B" or an hourly rate. At least half of each round's new phrasings carry a client or annual-pay term: in the 2026-10-13 inbox study the recruiters quoting the target named funded product companies and quoted annual salaries, while region and contract terms led to staff augmentation (`references/pay-tier-research.md`). Language: English first, at most one native-language phrasing per round (`seeds.language_rule`). Post phrasings work best as a recruiter would write the post: "hiring founding AI engineer LATAM", "Series A hiring LLM engineer remote anywhere". Give each subagent a mix of people and post phrasings.
   - **VC talent networks**: `<fund> talent partner` people searches for the funds in `vc_funds`, two or three funds per round, rotating through the list.
   - **Deeper pages** of the phrasings in `deeper`: result pages 3 to 6 of a productive phrasing usually still hold recruiters.
   - **Snowball**: people search `<company> recruiter` for each of `snowball_companies`, and the "People also viewed" rail on the profiles of the best leads.
   - **Hashtags**: post search for `#hiring <role term>`, `#<region> #hiring`, `#remotejobs <role term>`, with `sortBy=date_posted`.
   - **Job-post authors**: LinkedIn Jobs search for the target role filtered to remote (`f_WT=2`) in the candidate's country or region; the "Meet the hiring team" / "Posted by" person on each posting is a lead.
   - **Engagement on hiring posts**: the reposters and commenters on a hiring post for the target role are often recruiters at agencies placing the same role.
   - **Required terms**: every subagent's share includes at least one people and one post phrasing with each word in `required_terms` (for this candidate, `nearshore`: "nearshore recruiter LLM engineer", "hiring nearshore AI engineer LATAM USD", "recrutador nearshore engenheiro de IA"). Use configured ones first and invent fresh ones when they were run this week.
   Record invented phrasings exactly as run; `ingest` stores them in `discovery-runs.json`, so they join `queries_tried` and are not repeated within a week. A phrasing that returned nothing useful should not be repeated at all; note it in the round's one-line report so you don't invent it again.
   - **The company lane.** When `company_lane.short_by > 0`, give one of the round's subagents `company_targets` (the next `company_lane_per_round` companies, best first) on top of a smaller share of phrasings. It finds each company's in-house recruiter and hiring manager (`references/discover.md`, Company lane). `recruiters targets` shows the whole target list when you want to look first.
   - **Funding research.** When `funding_research.due` is true, run `recruiters funding-plan` and launch one more subagent in the same message as the Chrome subagents (`general-purpose`, `model: "sonnet"`): "Read `<rc>/references/funding.md` and follow it. Don't use Chrome." followed by the plan's JSON. It works on the open web (WebSearch, WebFetch), so it doesn't count toward `browser_parallel` and can't trip LinkedIn's limits. It finds software companies that raised recently or sit in VC portfolios and accelerator batches, and records each one's ATS board. When it finishes, run `recruiters ingest-funded --result <file>`, then `recruiters refresh-boards`, then `recruiters targets`; if the lane is still short, give the new targets to a lane subagent in the next round.
3. Split the list into `browser_parallel` groups (config.yaml, default 3) and launch them as Chrome subagents **in parallel, in one message** (`general-purpose`, `model: "sonnet"`). Chrome is not a serial resource: every Chrome tool takes a `tabId`, so each subagent creates its own tab in the shared tab group and works through the DOM tools (navigate, get_page_text, read_page, find, form_input, javascript_tool). Only `computer` screenshots see the window's visible tab, which is why the reference forbids them when running alongside others. Verified on 2026-10-10: two discovery subagents ran side by side with no cross-talk. Each prompt: "Read `<rc>/references/discover.md` and follow it. Other subagents are using Chrome at the same time: use only your own tab." followed by its share of `people_queries`, `post_queries`, `deeper`, `snowball_companies`, `vc_funds` and other avenues, `max_leads` (its share of the round's total), `known_profiles`, `reprobe`, `known_low_ceiling`, `target_role`, `pay_target_usd_month`, `pay_floor_usd_month`, `candidate`, `banned_companies`, `required_terms`, and its own `result` file (add a suffix per subagent). The subagent probes both profiles and feed posts: a recruiter's recent hiring posts are what answer the four questions.
4. As each one finishes, run `recruiters ingest --result <file>` and tell the user one line: leads added, `contactable_now` against `needed`, and the top few. Ingest stores, for every lead, the search that first found it (`source`) and every later one (`sources`). When it reports `required_terms_missing`, give the next pass those terms first. When it reports `leads_without_found_via`, tell that subagent (SendMessage) to fill in `found_via` and write the file again, because a lead without it can never tell you which search worked. If a subagent reports `blocked`, stop discovering for today. LinkedIn's own rate limits are per account, so keep the groups to `browser_parallel` and don't add more.
5. If `contactable_now` is still below `needed` after the round, run another discovery round with fresh phrasings and avenues before contacting, unless a round just added nothing at all from every avenue; in that case contact what there is and run one more round afterwards. Stop prospecting for the day only when `status` says `done`, LinkedIn blocked, or two consecutive rounds added no contactable lead.

## 2. Score

Scores are computed on ingest. Each lead gets up to 100 points from five signals. The rubric is the user's question, which is which recruiters have roles at the target pay. The weights come from the 2026-10-13 inbox study (`references/pay-tier-research.md`): region and activity predicted nothing about pay, while the client behind the recruiter predicted almost all of it.

| Question | Signal | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Explicitly hiring in the candidate's region and/or worldwide? | `hiring_scope`: the candidate's region (`latam` is the value's historical name) / worldwide 30, nearshore 15, remote with region unstated 12, unknown 4, local-only 0 | 30 |
| Active on LinkedIn? | `last_activity_days`: ≤7 → 10, ≤30 → 7, ≤90 → 4, older 0, no activity 2 | 10 |
| Posting roles aligned with the target role? | `role_posts_90d`: 2+ → 20, 1 → 13, only adjacent engineering roles → 5, none 0 | 20 |
| Posting roles aligned with the target pay? | `pay_signal`: at or above target 20, within 20% below 8 (an hourly `near` counts as below), no pay posted 5, below 0 | 20 |
| Recruiting for clients that pay it? | `client_tier`: funded product company or boutique search 20, unclear 8, staff augmentation / outsourcing / talent platform 0 | 20 |
| | `local_rate_shop` −15, `volume_vendor` (C2C/W2 US-IT vendors) −20, `task_platform` (annotation, AI-training, expert-network gigs) −20 | |

Leads probed before `client_tier`, `volume_vendor` and `task_platform` existed get them inferred from the headline, company and evidence (`infer_tier` in the script); an explicit signal from discovery always wins.

**The pay gate.** `<home>/recruiters/pay_ceilings.json` keeps, per company or agency, the highest monthly USD pay it ever quoted the user. When a lead's company ceiling, or the lead's own highest quote, is under `pay.skip_below_usd_month`, the lead is held at `min_score − 1` with a `gate` note, whatever else it scores. When it reaches `pay.target_usd_month`, the lead counts as posting target pay. One quote at the target lifts an agency out of the gate however many low ones it also made. Ceilings come from `recruiters outcome --event quoted` (linkedin-inbox records every pay quote it sees, from any recruiter, contacted or not), `recruiters import-quotes --file` (a backfill), and `recruiters ceilings --set <company> --usd N` (by hand); `recruiters ceilings` lists them.

A recruiter who answers yes to all five scores near 100; a nearshore staffing recruiter with no posted pay scores about 50, under the default `min_score` of 55. `recruiters score` recomputes everything after a settings or ceiling change and reports who left and who joined the pile. Leads marked irrelevant by the discovery subagent score 0 and are kept only so they aren't probed again.

**The company lane.** Recruiters were a proxy: every quote at the target traced back to a funded product company (`references/pay-tier-research.md`, Company lane). So the skill also goes to those companies directly. `company_targets` has two sources:

- **The job sweep's roles** from the last `company_lane_batches` sweeps, joined with job-autopilot's scores and the eligibility classifications. A company qualifies when it posted a role matching `company_lane_title_terms`, nothing marks the role closed to the candidate's region, and either its posted pay reaches the target or the posting came from a VC/YC portfolio or startup board with no pay under the floor.
- **The funded-company registry** (`funded_companies.json`), filled by the funding-research subagent from funding news, VC portfolio pages, accelerator batches and AI lists (`references/funding.md`). `refresh-boards` reads each registry company's Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever or Workable board through its public API on every run. It keeps postings whose title carries a target term and an engineering word, and labels each from the posting's own location and workplace fields: `open` to the region, `unclear` (remote, region unstated), `us_only`, `other_region` or `onsite`. A registry company becomes a target once it has an `open` or `unclear` matching role; until then it stays on the watch list.
- **job-autopilot's blocked tier A roles** (`autopilot/target_roles.json`). These are roles at a target-pay company
  with engineers in the candidate's region whose application form couldn't be filled, because of an account wall
  or load failures. The company is reached directly instead.

**The people check** (`<home>/people_checks.json`, shared with job-autopilot) counts as region evidence. A company
whose engineers already work in the candidate's country or region ranks as open to the region, whatever its
postings say. Posting labels are often wrong (user rule 2026-10-14).

Staffing names, talent marketplaces, banned companies and known low ceilings are left out of every source. Ranking: open to the candidate's region first, then posted pay at the target, then funded (a portfolio board, a round or a portfolio listing), then a round in the last `company_lane_funding_days`, then fit. Discovery finds the company's in-house recruiter (`lead_type: in_house`) and hiring manager (`hiring_manager`).

**Lane leads have their own rubric** (`lane_parts`, out of 100). The company is worth 40, from its posting: region open 15 / unclear 6, pay at the target 15 (funded with no posted pay 8), a matching open role 10. The person is worth 60: `role_owner` direct 30 / adjacent 15 / distant 0, activity up to 15, and `hiring_voice` (their own hiring posts) up to 15. Scoring the posting alone gave every lane lead 64-92, whoever the person was. A lane lead whose company has no matching open role is held below the line. Their contact line is `company_lane_min_score`. Lane and recruiter scores are never ranked against each other: `plan` fills `company_lane_share` of the connection and InMail slots from the lane's own pile and the rest from recruiters, and backfills unused slots from whichever lane still has leads.

Each lane task carries the company's open roles, its latest round when known, and whether job-autopilot applied there, so the note can name the role or the application. `recruiters queries` reports `by_lead_type`, which is the experiment: after two or three weeks, raise the share if the lane's `quoted_target` (or `positive`, since a hiring manager may not quote a number) beats recruiters, and lower it if it doesn't. `by_lane_origin` compares lane leads whose company came from the sweep with those from the registry.

**1st-degree network queues.** When the user runs a messaging round over their own network (a `{"queue": [...], "done": [...]}` file in `<home>/recruiters/`), run `recruiters network-rank [--file <name>]` first and again after each new ceiling import. It puts recruiters for funded product companies and boutique search first, unclear ones next, and staff augmentation, vendors, task platforms and known low-ceiling companies last. LinkedIn rate-limits messaging per account, so the order decides who gets reached at all.

## 3. Contact

1. Run `recruiters plan --n 40`. It returns today's quotas, `needed`, `company_lane` (the lane's reserved slots, how many it filled, and how many recruiters backfilled), the ranked pile of uncontacted leads at or above `min_score` cut to what today's budget can take (every contactable 1st-degree lead, plus up to `needed` others), `short_by` (how many more leads the connection and InMail budget could take than the pile holds; 1st-degree leads don't count), `first_degree_tasks`, `held_by_company_cap` (leads left out because their company already has `max_per_company` people contacted, or because the plan already holds the company's remaining slots), `accept_check` (connection requests not yet seen accepted, and the date to read new connections back to), plus the `result` path. Each task carries its `cta`, the closing style for that lead. Within a score band, 1st-degree connections come first because messaging them costs no connection request. They stay in the plan even when today's 20 requests are used. One company or agency never gets more than `max_per_company` people in total: the count includes everyone in `companies.json`, whatever run or channel contacted them.
2. Launch **one** Chrome subagent (`general-purpose`, `model: "sonnet"`) with the prompt: "Read `<rc>/references/message.md` and follow it." followed by the plan's `tasks` (each with its `cta`), `accept_check`, `quotas`, `candidate`, `banned_companies`, `contacts_file`, the exact `recruiters` command prefix, the fit-rubric path and the resume path, and `result`. Sending stays single on purpose: the daily counters in `pacing.json` are plain files without locking, so two senders could both pass `check` and overshoot the 20-request limit, and a sent message can't be taken back. **Discovery for the next round runs in parallel with it** whenever `short_by > 0`, so the next pile is ready when the sender finishes. The sender works down the pile: a plain message to every 1st-degree lead (no connection request spent, so this goes on after the 20 are used), connection requests with a custom note for the others until the 20 are used, then InMails while the compose window shows credits. It runs `recruiters check` before every send (which also refuses a lead whose company has reached `max_per_company`), writes each message for that recruiter from their evidence and tone, introduces the candidate in two lines, never states pay, and closes with the lead's `cta`: a plain statement that the candidate is open to new roles of this kind, or an offer to share the CV in the chat. It never asks for a call: a yes/no call request is easy to ignore and shuts out recruiters who screen by text, while an open door leaves the next step to the recruiter. Before sending, it reads the "Recently added" connections back to `accept_check.since`, which is how accepted requests get recorded. It stops only when the pile or the budget is empty; a pile that runs out with budget left is reported as `pile_empty: true`, which sends you back to step 1.
3. Run `recruiters record --result <file>`. It stores every contact with the exact message, its `cta` and the search that found the lead, updates the counters and the InMail credits hint, marks the leads contacted, and records `accepted` for every lead in `new_connections`. Leads the sender reports as `deferred` (or skipped only because the budget ran out) stay in the pile for the next day.
4. Run `recruiters status`. If `done` is false and nothing blocked, go back to step 1 (discover) or, when `contactable_now >= needed`, straight to a new plan. Skipped leads (already in conversation, Follow-only, wrong role) don't spend budget, so a round that skips many leads needs more discovery, not an early finish.

The run ends when `status` says `done: true`, when a subagent reports a block, when two consecutive discovery rounds found no contactable lead, or when the user says stop. Finishing with connection requests or InMail credits unused, or with 1st-degree leads in the pile left unmessaged, for any other reason is a failed run, and the report must say why.

**Replies** are handled by the linkedin-inbox skill, which also records them here: for every pending conversation it runs `recruiters outcome --profile <url> --name "<name>" --event replied`, plus `positive` when the recruiter asks for the CV, sends a role or offers a call, `call` when a call is booked, or `declined` when they say no. When a recruiter states the pay of a role, it also runs `--event quoted --usd <monthly USD> --company "<agency or employer>"`, for every recruiter, contacted by this skill or not. That quote is the outcome this skill optimises for, and it feeds the pay gate. When the user mentions that a recruiter answered, run the same command yourself. Events are idempotent and keep the earliest date, and a later stage implies the earlier ones (a reply implies the connection request was accepted).

## 4. Publish and report

1. Run `recruiters dashboard`. It writes `<home>/recruiters/dashboard.json`, which `jobsweep explorer` embeds in the role explorer's Outreach view (ranked leads with their score parts, evidence, recent roles, status, and the message sent).
2. Run the sweep's `jobsweep explorer` and republish the page to the same `explorer_url`, following job-source-sweep step 8e (read the artifact first, omit `capabilities`).
3. Run `recruiters queries`. It traces every lead to the search that first found it and shows the funnel per search, per avenue and per closing style: found, qualified (at or above `min_score`), contacted, accepted, replied, positive, declined, quoted (stated any pay) and quoted_target (stated the target pay or more), with acceptance and reply rates. Rows are ranked by quoted_target first: a search whose recruiters reply often but quote under the floor is filling the inbox, not the goal. `by_lead_type` compares recruiters, in-house recruiters and hiring managers on the same funnel (their `avg_score` comes from different rubrics; compare the outcome columns), and `by_lane_origin` compares the sweep's companies with the registry's. Report it every run, and recommend changing `company_lane_share` once a lane has five or more contacted leads. Rows with fewer than five contacted leads are flagged `small_sample`. It also counts the phrasings that were run but produced no lead.
4. Report: rounds run, leads discovered per avenue (configured phrasings, invented phrasings, deeper pages, snowball, hashtags, job posts) and the top of the pile; messages sent by channel and by `cta`; connection requests and InMail credits left (both should be zero; if not, the reason); leads skipped and deferred, and why; requests newly accepted; the dashboard link. From `recruiters queries`, name the searches whose recruiters quoted the target pay, then the ones with the best acceptance and reply rates once they have enough contacted leads, and the configured phrasings that keep producing leads nobody accepts or answers, or who only quote under the floor. Recommend adding the first kind to `config.yaml` and retiring the second. Until samples are large enough, rank by the share of qualified leads instead, and say so.

## Data it keeps

All in `<home>/recruiters/`: `leads.json` (every recruiter, signals, evidence, score, status, message sent, `source`/`sources` (the searches that found them) and `outcome` (accepted, replied, positive, call, declined, with dates)), `queries.json` (the last `recruiters queries` report), `contacts.json` (everyone ever contacted, including the old autopilot follow-ups), `pacing.json` (today's counters and credits, and when accepted requests were last checked), `pay_ceilings.json` (the highest monthly USD pay each company or agency ever quoted, with every quote; the pay gate), `funded_companies.json` (the company lane's registry: rounds, ATS board, matching roles with their region, when each was last checked), `companies.json` (people contacted per company or agency, rebuilt from `contacts.json` on every `record`; `recruiters companies` prints it with who is at the cap), `discovery-runs.json` (when each phrasing, configured or invented, last ran), `discovery-depth.json` (how many result pages of each phrasing were read), `work/<date>/` (result files). `recruiters status` summarises them.
